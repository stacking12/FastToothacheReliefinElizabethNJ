FAQs

Toothache & Emergency Dentist FAQs

Quick answers to common questions about dental emergencies and toothache relief.

What should I do if I have a severe toothache?

Rinse your mouth gently with warm water, floss to remove any trapped food, and use a cold compress on the outside of your cheek. Avoid placing aspirin directly on the gum. Then call us at 908-215-9958 so we can find and treat the cause of the pain.

When is a toothache a dental emergency?

Seek urgent care if you have severe or lasting pain, facial swelling, fever, a bad taste or a bump on the gum, bleeding that won't stop, or a tooth that was knocked out or broken. These can signal infection or serious damage that needs prompt treatment.

Do you offer same day emergency dental appointments?

Yes, we focus on urgent dental appointments and work to see emergency patients as quickly as possible. Call ahead so our team can prepare and tell you what to expect.

Will I need a root canal or an extraction?

It depends on the condition of the tooth. If the tooth can be saved, an emergency root canal is often the best option. If the damage or infection is too severe, an extraction may be recommended. We'll examine you and explain your choices before any treatment.

Is emergency dental care affordable?

We aim to keep emergency care affordable and transparent. Contact us to discuss your situation, and our team will explain treatment options and costs so there are no surprises.
